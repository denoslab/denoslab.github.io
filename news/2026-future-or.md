CALGARY. What if the emergency department could see what is coming? That was the question on the banner as University of Calgary researchers set up shop at Foothills Medical Centre on Tuesday to show health workers and innovators how artificial intelligence is being taught to do exactly that.

DENOS Lab members [Gerry Wu](/team/jiajun-wu/), [Alexander Burn](/team/alexander-burn/), and Samuel joined the Calgary Emergency Medicine Data Lab (CEMDL) at its booth for *The Future of the OR: A Health Everywhere Hub Living Lab & Innovation Event*, a day-long showcase in the HRIC Atrium organized by W21C, the health systems innovation centre based at the Cumming School of Medicine.

The two labs are co-developing multiple projects aimed at the front line of emergency care. CEMDL is directed by Dr. Jessalyn Holodinsky, an epidemiologist and emergency medicine researcher at the University of Calgary, and DENOS Lab is led by [Dr. Steve Drew](/team/steve-drew/) of the Schulich School of Engineering.

Three screens on the table showed three of those projects. The first plotted a year of forecasts from four machine learning models against the real number of patients who walked through the doors at Foothills Medical Centre, part of an effort to give departments advance warning of busy periods. The second demonstrated a system the team calls Priority-Conditioned Clinical Alignment, which learns the order in which clinicians rank competing priorities and blends them into a single score. The third ran [EDSim](/projects/edsim/), the lab's simulation of an emergency department floor in which autonomous agents play patients, nurses, and physicians so that staffing and triage changes can be tested before they reach a real patient.

Emergency departments across Alberta have been under sustained pressure, with long waits and crowded hallways a regular feature of the news. The researchers say the goal is not to replace the people doing the work but to give them a clearer picture of the shift ahead and a safe place to try changes.

The collaboration has already produced results. The EDSim team, which includes emergency physicians and researchers from both labs, has posted [*EDSim: An Agentic Simulator for Emergency Department Operations*](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-8960989/v1) as a preprint, and the University of Calgary [featured the work](https://cumming.ucalgary.ca/focus-impact/harnessing-data-research) earlier this year. Members of the two labs also co-authored the Starfish-FL paper on agentic federated analytics for healthcare, published in ACM Transactions on Computing for Healthcare in August.

Tuesday's event brought together clinicians, hospital staff, industry, and researchers under the Health Everywhere Hub banner to look at where operating rooms and acute care are heading. For the students at the booth it was a chance to put their systems in front of the people who would use them and hear what an emergency department needs before the next shift starts.
